    Frequently Asked Questions

    Interior design focuses on the aesthetic and functional arrangement of interior spaces—furniture, finishes, color, and lighting. Interior architecture goes further, involving the structural manipulation of space: moving walls, altering ceiling heights, and redesigning the built envelope. In practice, the two disciplines overlap significantly, and many designers work across both.

    Start with ambient (general) light on a dimmer, then add task lighting where you need it—reading, cooking, working. Avoid a single ceiling fixture. Use wall sconces, floor lamps, and table lamps to create layers. Warm color temperatures (2700K–3000K) make small spaces feel cozy rather than clinical. Mirrors and glossy surfaces can help bounce light around.

    Common pitfalls include: pushing all furniture against walls (creating a void in the center), ignoring scale (oversized or undersized pieces), using only one layer of lighting, neglecting negative space, and choosing trends over timelessness. Also, many people forget to plan storage—clutter undermines even the best design.

    Focus on reversible changes: removable wallpaper, freestanding storage, plug-in pendant lights, large rugs, and artwork. Swap cabinet hardware (keep the originals), add a freestanding kitchen island, and use tension rods for curtains. Good lighting—lamps, not overheads—transforms a space more than any paint color.

    Sustainable design minimizes environmental impact and supports human health. Start by choosing natural, low-VOC materials like solid wood, wool, linen, and clay paint. Buy vintage or locally made furniture. Prioritize durability over trends. Improve insulation and daylighting to reduce energy use. And don't forget: the most sustainable piece is the one you already own.
